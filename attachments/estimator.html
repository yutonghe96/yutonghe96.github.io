Intro

Splits monthly spare cash between paying down a Swedish mortgage and investing. Auto finds a good split by grid simulation; Manual lays out a plan year by year ("Apply to manual" fills it from an Auto result).

Model

1. Surplus

Budget minus required repayment, interest and avgift:

$$s_m = X_m - R_m - L_m\,\tfrac{r}{12} - A \tag{1}$$ $$X_m = X_0\,(1+\Delta X)^{\lfloor m/12\rfloor} \tag{2}$$

\(s_m\) surplus · \(X_m\) monthly budget (\(X_0\) at start), \(\Delta X\) its yearly change · \(L_m\) remaining loan (\(L_0\) original) · \(r\) interest rate / yr · \(A\) avgift / month · \(R_m\) required repayment (3).

2. Required repayment (Swedish rule)

$$R_m = \min\!\big(p\,L_0/12,\;L_m\big) \tag{3}$$ $$p = \begin{cases}2\% & L_m/V > 70\%\\ 1\% & L_m/V > 50\%\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} \tag{4}$$

\(p\) required repayment rate / yr, re-set yearly · \(V\) property value (\(L_0\) + down payment), so \(L_m/V\) is the loan-to-value (LTV).

\(R_m\) is the minimum: by (4) it steps down from 2 % to 1 % to 0 as the LTV falls, and anything paid beyond it (\(u_m\) in (5), \(\ell\) in (9)) is voluntary.

3. Strategy: prepay share \(\alpha\) while LTV > \(\theta\), invest the rest

$$u_m = \begin{cases}\min(\alpha\, s_m,\;L_m - R_m) & s_m > 0,\ (L_m - R_m)/V > \theta\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} \tag{5}$$ $$L_{m+1} = L_m - R_m - u_m \tag{6}$$ $$P_{m+1} = P_m\,G_m + (s_m - u_m) \tag{7}$$

\(\alpha\) share of surplus prepaid · \(\theta\) LTV at which prepaying stops · \(u_m\) prepaid (the rest, \(s_m - u_m\), is invested) · \(P_m\) savings · \(G_m\) monthly growth factor of savings (10).

A negative surplus \(s_m\) (1) is withdrawn from savings in (7); negative savings accrue the mortgage rate instead of \(G_m\). At each year end the interest deduction on that year's interest \(I_y = \sum_{m \in y} L_m\,r/12\) is refunded into savings:

$$D_y = \tau\,\min(I_y,\,100\text{k}) + \tau_2\,\max(I_y - 100\text{k},\,0) \tag{8}$$

\(\tau\) interest deduction (30 %) · \(\tau_2\) deduction on interest above 100k SEK / yr (21 %).

Optional year-end lump sum down to LTV \(\lambda\), keeping the buffer:

$$\ell = \max\!\big(0,\ \min(L_m - \lambda V,\; P_m - \text{buffer})\big) \tag{9}$$

\(\ell\) lump sum moved from savings to the loan · \(\lambda\) target LTV · buffer: savings always kept.

\(\theta\) and \(\lambda\) are separate rules on the same loan: \(\theta\) limits prepaying from each month's surplus (5), \(\lambda\) moves accumulated savings once a year (9), after the deduction refund (8). Because \(\lambda\) uses savings, which depend on the market path, the debt-free time varies across paths only when \(\lambda\) is set.

4. Market returns

$$G_m = \exp\!\Big(\tfrac{\ln(1+g)}{12} - \tfrac{\sigma^2}{24} + \tfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{12}}\, z_m\Big)\,(1-\kappa)^{1/12} \tag{10}$$

\(g\) expected return / yr · \(\sigma\) volatility / yr · \(\kappa\) investment tax / yr · \(z_m \sim \mathcal N(0,1)\) independent standard normal draw.

The \(-\sigma^2/24\) term in (10) makes \(\mathbb E[G_m] = \big((1+g)(1-\kappa)\big)^{1/12}\): \(g\) is the mean yearly return, \(\sigma\) the std. dev. of the yearly log return (0.15 ≈ ±15 %/yr; median return ≈ \(g - \sigma^2/2\)); \(\kappa\) is an ISK-style tax on the balance. All strategies share the same paths (fixed seed).

5. Simulation grid

The model draws many random return paths (10) and runs (1)–(9) on each path monthly. Each strategy then yields a distribution of outcomes across paths (net worth, debt-free times, shortfalls), which (11)–(15) summarise by quantiles. A fixed grid of 244 strategies is sampled: \(\alpha \in \{0, 0.05, \dots, 1\}\), \(\theta \in \{70, 50, 0\}\%\), \(\lambda \in \{\text{none}, 70, 50, 0\}\%\).

6. Outcome statistics

$$W = P_N - L_N \tag{11}$$ $$W_{\text{med}} = Q_{0.5}(W) \tag{12}$$ $$W_{\text{low}} = Q_{1-c}(W) \tag{13}$$ $$\hat t_F = Q_{c}(t) \tag{14}$$ $$\text{risk} = \Pr\big(\min_m P_m < \text{buffer}\big) \tag{15}$$

\(N\) horizon in months · \(W\) final net worth · \(c\) confidence · \(Q_q\) the \(q\)-quantile across simulated paths · \(W_{\text{med}}\) median ("med") and \(W_{\text{low}}\) pessimistic ("low 1%" at \(c\) = 99%) net worth · \(t\) first month debt-free · risk: share of paths where savings ever fall below the buffer (or below \(P_0\), if that starts lower).

Strategies with risk (15) above the max shortfall risk (default 1%) are hidden; nothing else is filtered. Confidence and max risk (the Filter section) only enter here, after the simulation, so changing them recomputes these statistics from the stored results instantly, without re-running. Horizon and paths (Grid setup) need a re-run.

7. Display

Corner plot: one point per shown strategy, over \(\alpha\), \(\theta\), \(\lambda\), shortfall risk (15), years to debt-free (14), \(W_{\text{med}}\) (12) and \(W_{\text{low}}\) (13). The diagonal shows each variable's distribution across strategies: a histogram for \(\theta\) and \(\lambda\) (titled max = most common level), a kernel density for the rest (titled max = the curve's peak, and σ = standard deviation). A strategy never debt-free within the horizon has no point on that axis. The highest \(W_{\text{low}}\) (13) is selected by default; click any point to inspect another.

Summary and band chart (selected strategy): final net worth as med and low; debt-free time as one value if every path agrees, otherwise med with the \(1-c\) to \(c\) band. The chart shows savings, loan and net worth per month: the med line and the \(1-c\) to \(c\) band.

Not modelled: the 4.5× income amortisation add-on, variable interest rates. Full notes: estimator_notes.md.
