Intro

Splits monthly spare cash between paying down a Swedish mortgage and investing. Auto finds a good split by grid simulation; Manual lays out a plan year by year ("Apply to manual" fills it from an Auto result).

Model

1. Surplus

Budget minus required repayment, interest and avgift:

$$s_m = X_m - R_m - L_m\,\tfrac{r}{12} - A \tag{1}$$ $$X_m = X_0\,(1+\Delta X)^{\lfloor m/12\rfloor} \tag{2}$$

Here \(s_m\) is the surplus in month \(m\), \(X_m\) the monthly budget (\(X_0\) at the start) and \(\Delta X\) its yearly change. \(L_m\) is the remaining loan (\(L_0\) the original), \(r\) the yearly interest rate (random from year 2 on, (10b)), \(A\) the monthly avgift, and \(R_m\) the required repayment (3).

2. Required repayment (Swedish rule)

$$R_m = \min\!\big(p\,L_0/12,\;L_m\big) \tag{3}$$ $$p = \begin{cases}2\% & L_m/V > 70\%\\ 1\% & L_m/V > 50\%\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} \tag{4}$$

Here \(p\) is the required yearly repayment rate, re-set each year, and \(V\) the property value (\(L_0\) plus the down payment), so \(L_m/V\) is the loan-to-value (LTV).

\(R_m\) is the minimum: by (4) it steps down from 2% to 1% to 0 as the LTV falls, and anything paid beyond it (\(u_m\) in (5), \(\ell\) in (9)) is voluntary.

3. Strategy: prepay share \(\alpha\) while LTV > \(\theta\), invest the rest

$$f_m = s_m - \min\!\big(\max(s_m, 0),\; \max(\text{buffer} - P_m G_m,\, 0)\big)$$ $$u_m = \begin{cases}\min(\alpha\, f_m,\;L_m - R_m) & f_m > 0,\ (L_m - R_m)/V > \theta\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} \tag{5}$$ $$L_{m+1} = L_m - R_m - u_m \tag{6}$$ $$P_{m+1} = P_m\,G_m + (s_m - u_m) \tag{7}$$

Here \(f_m\) is the surplus left after first refilling savings up to the buffer (the minimum payments in (1) always come first), \(\alpha\) the share of it prepaid, \(\theta\) the LTV at which prepaying stops, and \(u_m\) the amount prepaid (the rest, \(s_m - u_m\), is invested). \(P_m\) is savings and \(G_m\) their monthly growth factor (10).

A negative surplus \(s_m\) (1) is withdrawn from savings in (7); negative savings accrue the mortgage rate instead of \(G_m\). At each year end the interest deduction on that year's interest \(I_y = \sum_{m \in y} L_m\,r/12\) is refunded into savings:

$$D_y = \tau\,\min(I_y,\,100\text{k}) + \tau_2\,\max(I_y - 100\text{k},\,0) \tag{8}$$

Here \(\tau\) is the interest deduction (30%) and \(\tau_2\) the deduction on interest above 100k SEK a year (21%).

Optional year-end lump sum down to LTV \(\lambda\), keeping the buffer:

$$\ell = \max\!\big(0,\ \min(L_m - \lambda V,\; P_m - \text{buffer})\big) \tag{9}$$

Here \(\ell\) is the lump sum moved from savings to the loan, \(\lambda\) the target LTV, and the buffer the savings always kept.

\(\theta\) and \(\lambda\) are separate rules on the same loan: \(\theta\) limits prepaying from each month's surplus (5), \(\lambda\) moves accumulated savings once a year (9), after the deduction refund (8). The debt-free time varies across paths because the rate (10b) changes the surplus, and, when \(\lambda\) is set, because lump sums depend on market-driven savings.

4. Market returns

$$G_m = \exp\!\Big(\tfrac{\ln(1+g)}{12} - \tfrac{\sigma^2}{24} + \tfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{12}}\, z_m\Big)\,(1-\kappa)^{1/12} \tag{10}$$

Here \(g\) is the expected yearly return, \(\sigma\) the yearly volatility, \(\kappa\) the yearly investment tax, and \(z_m \sim \mathcal N(0,1)\) an independent standard normal draw.

The \(-\sigma^2/24\) term in (10) makes \(\mathbb E[G_m] = \big((1+g)(1-\kappa)\big)^{1/12}\): \(g\) is the mean yearly return, \(\sigma\) the std. dev. of the yearly log return (0.15 ≈ ±15%/yr; median return ≈ \(g - \sigma^2/2\)); \(\kappa\) is an ISK-style tax on the balance. All strategies share the same paths (fixed seed).

The mortgage rate is re-set once a year: year 1 uses the input rate \(r_0\), later years revert halfway back to it plus a random shock:

$$r_y = \max\!\big(0,\ x_y\big), \qquad x_y = r_0 + \phi\,(x_{y-1} - r_0) + \sigma_r\, z'_y, \qquad \phi = 0.5 \tag{10b}$$

Here \(\sigma_r\) is the rate volatility (the yearly shock's std. dev., in rate units: 0.01 ≈ ±1 percentage point/yr, so from 3% the next year's rate is typically 2–4%, and in the long run about two years in three stay within 1.85–4.15%) and \(z'_y\) an independent standard normal, drawn separately from the market's \(z_m\). The default \(\sigma_r = 0.01\) roughly matches Swedish variable mortgage rates since 2005, which ranged from about 1.4% to 5.5%.

5. Simulation grid

The model draws many random return paths (10) and runs (1)–(9) on each path monthly. Each strategy then yields a distribution of outcomes across paths (net worth, debt-free times, lowest savings), which (11)–(15) summarise by quantiles. A fixed grid of 244 strategies is sampled: \(\alpha \in \{0, 0.05, \dots, 1\}\), \(\theta \in \{70, 50, 0\}\%\), \(\lambda \in \{\text{none}, 70, 50, 0\}\%\).

6. Outcome statistics

$$W = P_N - L_N \tag{11}$$ $$W_{\text{med}} = Q_{0.5}(W) \tag{12}$$ $$W_{\text{low}} = Q_{1-c}(W) \tag{13}$$ $$t_f = Q_{c}(t) \tag{14}$$ $$P_{\text{low}} = Q_{1-c}\big(\min_m P_m\big) \tag{15}$$

Here \(N\) is the horizon in months, \(W\) the final net worth, \(c\) the confidence, and \(Q_q\) the \(q\)-quantile across simulated paths. \(W_{\text{med}}\) is the median ("med") and \(W_{\text{low}}\) the pessimistic ("low 1%" at \(c\) = 99%) net worth. \(t\) is the first month debt-free on a path, and \(t_f\) its \(c\)-quantile. \(P_{\text{low}}\) is the pessimistic lowest savings: on each path take the lowest savings in any month, then its \(1-c\) quantile. Because the surplus refills the buffer first (5), savings rarely stay below it, so the chance of breaking the buffer says little; \(P_{\text{low}}\) instead shows how much cash a strategy keeps on hand: investing keeps more, prepaying and lump sums keep it near the buffer.

All strategies are shown. Confidence (the Filter section) only enters here, after the simulation, so changing it recomputes these statistics from the stored results instantly, without re-running. Horizon and paths (Grid setup) need a re-run.

7. Display

Corner plot: one point per shown strategy, over \(\alpha\), \(\theta\), \(\lambda\), \(P_{\text{low}}\) (15), \(t_f\) (14), \(W_{\text{med}}\) (12) and \(W_{\text{low}}\) (13). The diagonal shows each variable's distribution across strategies: a histogram for \(\theta\) and \(\lambda\) (titled Mo = mode, the most common level), a kernel density for the rest (titled Mo = mode, the curve's peak, and σ = standard deviation). A strategy never debt-free within the horizon has no point on that axis. The highest \(W_{\text{low}}\) (13) is selected by default; click any point to inspect another.

All strategies: each shown strategy's median savings, loan and net worth per month, one faint line each, with the selected strategy drawn bold. Click a line to select that strategy.

Summary and band chart (selected strategy): final net worth as med and low; debt-free time as one value if every path agrees, otherwise med with the \(1-c\) to \(c\) band; lowest savings as med and low (15). The chart shows savings, loan and net worth per month: the med line and the \(1-c\) to \(c\) band.
